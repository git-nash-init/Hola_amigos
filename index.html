Meet Your Guide

Snehal Suryawanshi

Founder, Hola Amigos | Adventure Travel Professional | Certified Mountaineer | Trek Leader

The Journey That Made Me Want to Create Journeys for Others

There was a time when travel, for me, simply meant going somewhere new. A new place. A new view. A new photograph.

But somewhere along the way, travel became much more than that. It became the feeling of standing on top of a mountain after a tiring climb and realizing, "I actually did it." It became the conversations with strangers who slowly became friends. It became waking up before sunrise just to watch the sky change colours. It became getting lost on unfamiliar roads, discovering places that weren't on the usual tourist list, trying local food, listening to stories about a place, and coming back home with memories that no photograph could completely capture.

I Didn't Just Travel. I Discovered Myself.

Every journey has given me something. Some trips gave me adventure. Some gave me peace. Some challenged me physically. Some pushed me out of my comfort zone. And some simply gave me a few days where I could forget about deadlines, responsibilities and the endless routine of everyday life.

Looking back, I realize that the destination was only half the story. The journey was the real experience. And that's exactly how I want other people to travel.

From a Traveller to a Founder

Starting my own company is honestly a little scary and incredibly exciting. I'm doing it because I genuinely love travel. I'm doing it because I believe experiences are worth investing in. I'm doing it because I know how a single trip can change your mood, your perspective, your confidence, sometimes even your life.

And Yes, I'm a Woman Starting This Journey

As a woman, I've learned that sometimes we think twice before taking that first step. I've had those thoughts too. But I've also learned that confidence doesn't always come before you start. Sometimes, confidence comes because you started. Every mountain I've climbed has taught me that the first step is rarely the hardest one. The hardest step is often deciding that you're going to take it.

"You don't have to have everything figured out. You just have to be brave enough to begin."
